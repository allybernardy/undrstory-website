the work, in practice

these are real situations. no one in them was intentionally ignoring what was there. it's usually an undercurrent — running quietly beneath the surface, shaping everything, waiting to be acknowledged.

they were carrying something real inside a fast-moving company. they could see the problems clearly — and kept trying to surface them. reframing them, presenting them differently, finding the angle that might finally make someone care. maybe this format. maybe this meeting. maybe this time.

every week they left a little more defeated.

it wasn't going to shift. not because they were wrong — they weren't. but the capacity to see it, and the will to fix it, simply wasn't there.

once they could see that clearly, they stopped trying to change it. they left. six months later they were giddy.

the colleague who stayed is still in it.

sometimes the truth is: this isn't going to change. and knowing that — really knowing it — is the thing that sets you free.

two people building something together. same words, same stated vision. but every time they left a meeting, they were walking toward different things — and one of them kept naming it.

I don't think we're holding the same vision. I think we should part ways.

three times: no. let's keep trying.

the split happened anyway — forced by the one party who hadn't been heard. messier than it needed to be.

the longer the truth is denied, the longer you wait for the other party to catch up. the more defeated you feel.

three months later: I finally see what you were saying.

the truth doesn't stop being true because someone isn't ready for it yet. but you're the one waiting — and you're the one who feels it.

the highest-priority project in the company. a strict deadline. and every week, the person championing its importance was focused elsewhere — on minor projects that filled their plate but had no bearing on what actually mattered right now.

it got named directly: if this is the priority you say it is, I need you to hand these off and focus on the real one. I need you here.

defensiveness first. then, within days: you're right. I'm here.

the goal was achieved. the deadline hit.

when someone is ready — when the conditions exist for the truth to land — things move fast. that's what this work is for.

the fault line was visible from week three. two teams whose work was deeply symbiotic, operating in near-total isolation. broken from the start.

the concerns got raised — in meetings, in writing, with specific questions that needed answers.

that's a really well-articulated question. I need to digest it and get back to you.

they never got back.

months later, the proof arrived — a founder stepping down from their executive role. the exact pressure point named at the beginning. the response: it was honorable. this is normal for scaling companies.

they couldn't acknowledge that the cracks their team had been quietly holding together for months were the same ones named from the start. the truth had arrived. it just couldn't be spoken out loud.

the question was never just: is this true? it was: are they ready to act on it? can they acknowledge it even when it arrives? and what do you want to do, once you know?

the truth has its own timeline. are you ready?

in a forest, the understory holds more life than the canopy above it. the most important growth has always happened beneath what's visible.
